# WeWork V2 Product Design

Status: current product baseline.

## 1. Product Definition

WeWork is a desktop team operating interface for creating digital employees, assigning work, observing responsibility, and retaining reusable team knowledge.

The product is not a Employee gallery, chat application, or progress dashboard. Its primary question is: **what work is waiting, who owns it, and what can the user do next?**

V2 must support four complete loops:

1. Create a team with a first employee and a default configuration.
2. Add employees with independent runtime adapters, roles, and optional embedded skills.
3. Drag pending work onto an employee and observe acceptance, execution, and output.
4. Inspect an employee's work, context, artifacts, and runtime state without exposing harness-specific internals.

Handoff, offboarding, shared-context governance, and external project tools remain planned capabilities, but must not dominate the competition prototype.

## 2. Product Principles

- Direct manipulation before configuration: users assign work by dragging it to an employee.
- Progressive disclosure: the active team view stays visually quiet; detail appears only after selection.
- Spatial consistency: employees remain on one table ring across top-down, transition, and eye-level views.
- Employee equals Session: an employee is the persistent object users message, assign, pause, and inspect. Persona is configuration, not a separate user-facing entity.
- Runtime per employee: Pi, Claude Code, DSH, or another adapter is chosen when the employee is created.
- Skills are optional: an employee may start without embedded skills and learn or package reusable skills later.
- Paperwork and Digital Work are metadata on one pending-work flow, not separate workspaces.
- Team progress is not a permanent percentage. Teams continue after individual work completes.
- Context is selective and bounded. Group activity, Session context, team knowledge, and Workspace are different resources.
- Empty space is functional. The default screen shows only controls needed for the next action.

## 3. Desktop Information Architecture

V2 targets desktop only. No portrait or mobile layout is designed.

### Team View Modes

A team selects one primary visual topology:

- `Round table`: member-oriented collaboration and direct assignment.
- `Fixed workflow`: work-oriented execution through a role DAG.

The two views share the same Team, Employee, WorkItem, Run, Artifact, and Runtime records. Switching topology changes presentation and assignment policy, not the underlying employee identities or work history.

### Left: Team Navigation

- Product identity.
- Create-team command.
- Team list with name, status marker, up to three employee color markers, and `+N` overflow.
- No fabricated progress bars or completion ratios.

### Center: Round-table WeWork

The round table is the primary team surface.

- The tabletop is white and distinguished only by restrained shadow and depth.
- Employees occupy evenly distributed seats outside the table edge.
- Only the hovered employee enlarges.
- An available seat uses the same size, projection, hover, focus, and ring behavior as an employee, but renders as a gray circle with a large plus.
- Employee color is assigned by the system by default.
- Labels show only employee name, role, and a concise state when needed.

#### Top-down behavior

- Default overview state.
- First click selects an employee without changing seat ownership.
- Clicking the selected employee starts the camera transition to eye level.
- Clicking empty stage space clears focus and returns to the overview.
- A plus seat follows the same selection model; it opens employee creation only after becoming focused.
- Production UI does not expose a permanent top-down/eye-level toggle.

#### Eye-level behavior

- The camera lands above the tabletop and faces the selected employee across the table.
- The tabletop remains visible at the bottom of the scene.
- Adjacent visible employees remain consistent with the same physical ring.
- Employees behind the camera are not rendered; edge employees fade out.
- Selecting another employee moves the camera around the table in the same spatial direction.
- Clicking the focused employee opens the employee workbench.
- Two-person teams may place both employees on the far side for a coherent visible composition.

### Center Alternative: Fixed Workflow DAG

The round table may be replaced by a fixed-process view when work follows a repeatable role sequence.

- WeWork selects a versioned workflow template from the incoming work type, or the user selects one explicitly.
- DAG nodes represent roles or processing stages, not individual screen widgets.
- Each active node resolves to one responsible employee whose capabilities and runtime satisfy the role requirement.
- Directed edges express dependency and handoff order.
- Parallel branches express work that may proceed concurrently; join nodes wait for required upstream outputs.
- The current node receives the strongest emphasis. Completed and future nodes remain quiet.
- Selecting a node reveals its responsible employee, current WorkItem, required inputs, produced outputs, and blocker.
- Dragging pending work onto the DAG starts the selected workflow after showing the resolved role-to-employee assignment.
- Employee replacement changes node ownership without rewriting the workflow definition.

The DAG is not a general-purpose visual programming canvas in V2. Users choose or inspect a fixed workflow; arbitrary graph editing is deferred.

### Pending Work

Pending work is one collapsible control near the upper-right of the main wework, not a permanent bottom tray or a separate right-side dashboard.

- Collapsed state shows only the pending count and attention state.
- Expanded state shows a looping cover-flow of pending cards.
- Cards carry a `Paperwork` or `Digital` type marker as metadata.
- Dragging a card over an eligible employee creates a strong acceptance-focus state.
- Dropping the card assigns ownership, animates it into the employee, and updates the employee's work summary.
- A toast is secondary confirmation and dismisses automatically.

### Team Management

Team management is a separate, discoverable overlay opened from the wework header. It is not another permanent page column.

Default modules:

- Team identity and purpose.
- Lead employee and member responsibilities.
- Team Context summary and policy.
- Runtime/provider health.

Optional modules:

- Lightweight WorkItem board.
- Activity/audit projection.
- Connected external planning or knowledge tools.

Optional modules are hidden when disabled. Their absence must not prevent basic team operation.

### Employee Workbench

The employee workbench opens as a large desktop overlay.

Primary area:

- Work conversation and guidance.
- Compact, expandable Session-context summary within the conversation surface.
- Current and queued WorkItems with priority, blocker, and latest result.
- Message composer and attachments when runtime messaging is supported.

Secondary area:

- Employee identity and role.
- Runtime connection state and capability summary.
- Embedded skills and work the employee can perform.
- Artifacts and output provenance.

Low-frequency actions stay collapsed or in a menu:

- Pause or resume.
- Handoff selected work.
- Export/import a context package.
- Remove employee from team.

The UI must show `Runtime not connected` when no backend adapter exists. It must not claim `Workspace Server` or another harness is active without a real connection.

## 4. Creation Flows

### Team Creation

Quick creation uses defaults and asks only for information required to start:

1. Team name and purpose.
2. Default team template.
3. First employee name and role.
4. Runtime selection only when no default runtime profile exists.
5. Review and create.

The first employee becomes the default lead. Advanced context, model, permission, and Workspace settings remain collapsed.

A draft may exist while runtime validation fails, but an unusable team is not shown as active.

### Additional Employee Creation

Entry point: a gray plus seat on the round-table ring.

Required fields:

- Name and role.
- Runtime adapter or inherited team default.

Optional fields:

- Description.
- Embedded skill bundle.
- Model, Workspace, permission, and context overrides.

The system assigns color automatically. Adapter-specific configuration comes from server-owned capability descriptors, and secrets remain server-side.

## 5. Work and Context Model

### Work hierarchy

- `PendingWork`: a unified UI item carrying Paperwork/Digital metadata.
- `Paperwork`: team-level incoming work.
- `WorkItem`: an employee-owned unit created by assignment or decomposition.
- `Run`: one execution attempt for a WorkItem.
- `OutputRecord`: responsible employee, contributors, summary, artifacts, and review state.
- `Artifact`: a versioned output linked to its WorkItem and Run.

Every WorkItem has one accountable employee. Reassignment preserves ownership history.

### Context boundaries

- `TeamContext`: accepted team brief, decisions, reusable references, milestone summaries, and shared artifacts.
- `Session context`: finite information currently loaded by one employee.
- `Workspace`: execution environment and artifact boundary; it is not model context.
- `Group activity`: collaboration event stream; it is not injected wholesale into every employee.

At Run start, context is composed from:

1. Employee role and optional embedded skills.
2. Assigned WorkItem and acceptance criteria.
3. Explicit dependencies.
4. Authorized TeamContext fragments.
5. Selected artifacts and prior Session summary.

Unrelated board cards, other employees' private Sessions, raw traces, and unrestricted team history are excluded.

Completed work may create a `ContextProposal`. Accepted proposals become versioned TeamContext; raw output remains owned by its responsible employee.

## 6. Runtime Adapter Boundary

Each employee references one replaceable `AgentRuntime` and a registered adapter.

Normalized capabilities include:

- Provision and health check.
- Create/resume Session.
- Start, observe, pause, resume, cancel, or retry Run when supported.
- Send message when supported.
- List artifacts.
- Export/import selected context for handoff when supported.

Capability negotiation is mandatory. Unsupported actions are hidden rather than simulated.

Initial adapter targets:

- Pi.
- Claude Code.
- DSH.
- Workspace task adapter, explicitly marked as limited when appropriate.

Harness-specific payloads, command templates, API keys, and host paths never enter the shared browser domain model.

## 7. Domain Invariants

- An active Team has at least one employee.
- An active Team has exactly one ready lead employee.
- Employee identity survives runtime replacement.
- One WorkItem has one accountable employee.
- Progress boards project WorkItem state; they do not own duplicate task state.
- Team Context changes are versioned and attributable.
- Removing an employee never deletes work, artifacts, or audit history.
- UI state never claims a runtime capability that the backend has not reported.

## 8. Delivery Scope

### Competition prototype

1. Create a team from defaults.
2. Add an employee from the plus seat.
3. Configure each employee's runtime adapter.
4. Expand pending work and drag a card to an employee.
5. Show dispatch acceptance and working-state feedback.
6. Open the employee workbench and inspect work, context, runtime state, and artifacts.
7. Preserve the spatial top-down to eye-level interaction.
8. Demonstrate one fixed role-DAG workflow as an alternative team topology.

Explicitly deferred:

- Team group chat.
- Complex progress dashboards and team completion percentages.
- Multi-team/sub-team orchestration.
- Full external board and knowledge-provider integrations.
- Arbitrary workflow graph authoring.
- Cross-runtime handoff rollback.
- Mobile and portrait layouts.

### Later phases

1. Persist TeamContext and selective ContextGrants.
2. Add optional WorkItem board and external provider adapters.
3. Add handoff, offboarding, and successor verification.
4. Add review workflows, audit, retention, and operational controls.

## 9. Visual and Motion Requirements

- The page uses restrained structure and meaningful whitespace, not a wall of cards.
- The round table is white with shadow; no wood texture, gradient decoration, or decorative rings.
- Employee SVGs scale proportionally with their seats, including H-frame stroke.
- Motion communicates workflow state and camera movement only.
- Idle is nearly still; working never uses continuous spinning.
- Spatial transitions use Three.js camera movement rather than independent CSS layout morphs.
- Reduced-motion mode preserves final geometry without transitional movement.

Detailed motion and camera contracts live in the supporting design documents.
